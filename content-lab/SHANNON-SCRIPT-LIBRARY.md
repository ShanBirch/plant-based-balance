- Add a script only after Shannon approves its spoken wording.
- Preserve the approved script exactly. Put later production notes, visual pairings and lessons outside the script block.

- Before drafting, read at least the two closest entries when the library contains enough relevant examples. Compare sentence construction, contractions, transitions, explanation length, cue language and CTA shape.

## EXERCISE-001 — Hanging leg raises: three mistakes

- Approved: 2026-08-01
- Format: Exercise Reel / three mistakes / COACH
- Exercise: Hanging leg raise
- Status: Approved spoken script; production revision pending
- CTA keyword: `BALANCE`
- Primary voice lesson: Explain the mechanism in ordinary gym language, then give a physical picture the viewer can recognise.

### Approved script

> Hanging leg raises aren’t automatically a six-pack exercise.
>
> So watch this and make sure you’re actually doing them right.
>
> Okay, here’s three mistakes.
>
> Mistake one.
>
> First of all, you’re only thinking about pulling your legs up.
>
> Pulling your legs up works your hip flexors.
>
> It doesn’t necessarily mean your abs are doing much.
>
> What you wanna understand is, it’s not really about how high your legs go.
>
> It’s about getting your hips closer to your rib cage.
>
> So at the top, if someone was standing in front of you, they should be able to see your bum.
>
> Mistake two: you’re swinging too much.
>
> If you swing out of the bottom, your momentum’s doing the work.
>
> That’s why I’ve got the dumbbell there.
>
> I tap it, stop, then go again.
>
> Mistake three: you’re not controlling the eccentric—the way down.
>
> Don’t just let your legs fall.
>
> As your hips move away from your ribs, fight it.
>
> Slow that bit down.
>
> The second your lower back starts to arch, that’s far enough.
>
> If you can’t do it with straight legs, bend your knees.
>
> That’s fine.
>
> This is what I do with my clients inside Balance.
>
> I watch how you’re moving, work out where it’s going wrong, then change the exercise to suit you.
>
> Comment BALANCE below if you want me in your corner.

### Meaning-matched visual map

| Spoken beat | Required visual |
| --- | --- |
| `Pulling your legs up works your hip flexors.` | Keep the real rep moving and show the hip flexors. The anatomy must clarify that lifting the thighs is hip flexion. |
| `It’s about getting your hips closer to your rib cage.` | Switch to the abs/rectus abdominis visual and show the ribs-to-pelvis relationship. Do not reuse the hip-flexor visual for this line. |
| `They should be able to see your bum.` | Show or briefly hold the real top position where the pelvis has tucked underneath. The body cue is the proof. |
| `That’s why I’ve got the dumbbell there.` | Show the live foot tap on the dumbbell: tap, stop, then start the next rep. |
| `You’re not controlling the eccentric—the way down.` | Show the real lowering phase moving continuously. Do not replace it with a static anatomy hold. |
| `Comment BALANCE below...` | Full-screen Shannon/avatar with the visible `COMMENT BALANCE` card. |

### What this entry teaches future drafts

- Shannon naturally says `what you wanna understand is`, `first of all`, `that’s why I’ve got...`, `slow that bit down`, and `that’s fine` when those phrases genuinely connect the thought.
- A concrete physical picture sounds more human than a compressed cue. `Someone standing in front of you should be able to see your bum` is better than `pelvis towards ribs`.
- Technical language is acceptable when it is immediately translated: `the eccentric—the way down`.
- Use contractions everywhere they occur naturally: `aren’t`, `you’re`, `doesn’t`, `it’s`, `that’s`, `I’ve`, `don’t`, and `can’t`.
- Visual anatomy must change with the mechanism being explained. Hip flexors illustrate lifting the legs; abs illustrate bringing the hips and ribs closer.
