Start here

Testosterone is not a fat-loss drug.

Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) is designed to restore testosterone to a normal physiological range in someone with symptoms and repeatedly low blood levels. It is not an approved shortcut for getting lean, and taking extra testosterone when your level is already normal is not “replacement.”

If the goal is simply a cut, the first medical conversation should be about body composition, nutrition, sleep, training, medications and evidence-based weight management—not automatically adding an anabolic hormone.

The biology

What testosterone actually does

Testosterone is an androgen hormone. In men, most is made in the testes after signals from the brain and pituitary. It affects sexual function, sperm production, bone, red blood cells, muscle, fat distribution, mood and other tissues.

01

Brain & pituitary

The hypothalamus and pituitary send LH and FSH signals that control testosterone and sperm production.

02

Muscle & bone

Androgen-receptor signalling supports protein synthesis, lean mass, bone density and recovery of normal function when deficiency is present.

03

Sexual function

Normal testosterone contributes to libido, erections and sperm production—but injected testosterone can reduce or stop sperm production.

04

Conversion

Some testosterone converts to DHT and some to estradiol. Both have useful roles and can also contribute to side effects.

The trade-off

Potential benefits and real downsides

The benefit side is strongest when someone genuinely has hypogonadism. It cannot be assumed from tiredness, low mood or a single low result, because those can have many causes.

What may improve with confirmed deficiency

  • Libido and some aspects of sexual function
  • Lean mass and strength response
  • Bone density over time
  • Low red-cell count caused by deficiency
  • Energy, mood or wellbeing in some men
  • Maintenance of normal male sex characteristics

Response varies. “Normalising a deficiency” and “taking extra for performance” are not the same evidence base.

Risks and common trade-offs

  • Reduced sperm production, infertility and smaller testes
  • Raised haematocrit (“thicker” blood) and clot concern
  • Higher blood pressure and fluid retention
  • Acne, oily skin and accelerated male-pattern hair loss
  • Breast tenderness or gynaecomastia
  • Worsened sleep apnoea or urinary symptoms
  • Mood changes; abuse can cause serious psychiatric effects
  • Injection pain, bleeding, infection, abscess or nerve injury

Long-term safety questions remain. Recent indicated-use trial data did not show more major heart attacks or strokes, but pulmonary embolism and fracture signals still require attention.

Dose reality

There is no universal “safe dose”

Risk depends on the exact ester and formulation, concentration, injection interval, starting hormone level, age, blood pressure, haematocrit, fertility goals, medical history and the blood level produced—not just the milligrams on the vial.

mg ≠ mg

A large number may be a long-acting dose given every 10–14 weeks, while a smaller number may be a short-acting weekly dose. Injectable products are not automatically interchangeable milligram-for-milligram.

Lower-risk context

Replacement, not enhancement

Confirmed deficiency, regulated prescription product, a formulation-specific plan, levels kept in the normal target range and scheduled monitoring.

Higher risk

Unmonitored or unclear

One blood test, no symptom-based diagnosis, no fertility discussion, uncertain product strength, borrowed dosing, missed blood pressure or haematocrit checks.

Dangerous territory

Supraphysiologic use

Escalating until levels remain above the normal range, “cycling,” stacking with other anabolic steroids, using illicit products or continuing despite adverse results. There is no scientifically valid milligram line below which this becomes safe.

Injection routes

Into muscle vs under the skin

Both routes exist, but route and site belong to the exact medicine—not to testosterone in general. A product labelled for deep intramuscular use should not be casually moved into fat, and a subcutaneous auto-injector should not be pushed into muscle.

IM

Intramuscular

The medicine is deposited deeper into a large muscle, where the oil-based ester releases over time.

  • Where: often the gluteal muscle; some products or clinician protocols may use another large muscle.
  • Used for: cypionate, enanthate, mixed esters and long-acting undecanoate, depending on the exact label.
  • Trade-offs: deeper injection, soreness, peaks and troughs with some short-acting schedules.
Some long-acting oil injections must be given in a clinic. Never inject testosterone into a vein.
SC / SubQ

Subcutaneous

The medicine is deposited in the fatty layer beneath the skin. It can be comfortable and produce steadier weekly exposure with a product designed and tested for this route.

  • Where: one approved testosterone-enanthate auto-injector specifies the left or right abdomen, avoiding the area close to the navel.
  • Used for: formulation-specific products; not every oil vial is labelled or tested for subcutaneous use.
  • Trade-offs: redness, bruising, bleeding, firmness or discomfort at the site can occur.
Have the prescriber or pharmacist demonstrate the exact technique for the exact device, site and formulation. A web guide cannot safely substitute for that training.

How doctors use it

Diagnosis first. Monitoring always.

Professional guidelines diagnose testosterone deficiency only when relevant symptoms occur alongside unequivocally and consistently low levels. The usual starting point is at least two early-morning fasting tests on separate days.

Before treatment

1
Confirm the patternSymptoms, medical and medication history, sleep, weight, training load, alcohol and other reversible causes.
2
Repeat morning labsTotal testosterone on two separate mornings; free testosterone when clinically indicated.
3
Find the causeLH, FSH and sometimes prolactin or further pituitary/testicular investigation.
4
Check safetyBlood count/haematocrit, blood pressure, fertility plans, sleep apnoea, clot risk and prostate assessment based on age and risk.

After starting

1
Review responseAre the original symptoms improving? Any acne, mood, urinary, sleep, breast or injection issues?
2
Measure at the right timeTestosterone sampling is timed to the formulation—often a trough or midpoint for injections.
3
Track blood and pressureRepeat haematocrit, haemoglobin and blood pressure after initiation and periodically thereafter.
4
Change course when neededA haematocrit above 54% generally triggers stopping or reducing therapy and evaluation for contributors such as hypoxia or sleep apnoea.

Do not wait

Get urgent medical help for these symptoms

Call 000 in Australia for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms. After an injection, sudden breathing or allergic symptoms can be time-critical.

Chest pain, severe shortness of breath or fainting
Sudden cough, throat tightness, sweating or dizziness during/after an oil injection
One-sided leg swelling, warmth or pain
Face droop, weakness, speech trouble or a sudden severe headache
Swelling of the face/tongue, hives or trouble breathing
A hot, spreading, very painful injection site with fever

The sensible next move

If you are considering it, take the goal—not a pre-selected dose—to a GP.

Be honest about the goal, whether that is fat loss, low libido, fatigue, fertility, strength or something else. Ask for a proper evaluation before buying or injecting anything. In Australia, testosterone is prescription-only, and possession or supply without a prescription or permit is illegal.

A few direct answers

Common questions

This page is general education. A doctor who knows your history and sees your examination and blood results is the person who can tell you whether treatment is appropriate.

Will testosterone make me shredded?

Not by itself. Correcting genuine deficiency can improve lean mass and training response, but testosterone is not an approved fat-loss treatment. Extra testosterone may also cause fluid retention, appetite changes and side effects. Getting lean still depends heavily on energy intake, training, recovery and the reason body fat is high.

Does a “low dose” mean low risk?

No. “Low” has no meaning without the formulation, interval, starting level and blood response. A dose that looks modest can still push one person above the normal range, raise haematocrit, increase blood pressure or shut down fertility.

Can natural testosterone production recover after stopping?

It often can, but the timing and completeness are unpredictable. Recovery may take months and can be incomplete, especially after longer or higher-dose exposure. Fertility can remain suppressed. This is a reason to discuss future children before starting, not after.

Is subcutaneous testosterone safer than intramuscular testosterone?

Not automatically. It may be easier to administer and can create smoother exposure with an appropriate product, but it still has systemic testosterone risks. Route-specific safety depends on the medicine, dose, technique and monitoring.
