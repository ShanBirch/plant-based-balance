# Scientific wording proposals for Shannon, 16 September 2026

**Review only. None of these changes have been applied to the course or quizzes.** The requested original restoration is exact. These are narrow proposed corrections to overstatements in the originals, not a replacement course. If adopted, the corresponding quiz answer and feedback would need a separately reviewed matching edit.

| Lesson | Claim to reconsider | Proposed exact replacement wording |
|---|---|---|
| mind-1-2 | Only prediction violations reach consciousness | “Your brain combines expectations with sensory evidence. Both expected and unexpected sensations can become conscious.” |
| mind-1-3 | Prediction error is the only learning mechanism; surprise forces an update | “Prediction errors can guide learning. Whether an experience changes an expectation depends on how the evidence is interpreted and weighted.” |
| mind-3-2 | Constructed emotion presented as settled, exclusive fact | “The theory of constructed emotion proposes that the brain uses bodily signals, context and learned concepts to construct emotional experiences.” |
| mind-3-5 | Every positive experience updates expectations | “Positive experiences can provide evidence for new expectations. Change is not guaranteed after every experience.” |
| mind-4-2 | Frequency matters, intensity does not | “Repeated practice can help learning. Frequency, intensity, context and the outcome of the experience can all matter.” |
| mind-4-5 | Missing a day teaches that a behaviour is optional | “A streak records repeated behaviour. Missing a day does not erase previous learning; returning is part of building a sustainable routine.” |
| mind-6-1 | Self-control does not exist and is entirely competing predictions | “Self-control describes processes that help regulate behaviour. Expectations and context can influence those processes; this does not mean self-control is unreal.” |
| mind-6-2 | Every conscious decision merely narrates a completed process | “Active inference offers a framework for understanding action through expectations, goals and uncertainty. It does not establish that conscious planning has no influence.” |
| mind-7-1 | The brain has one proved function; surprise equals a simple gap | “The free-energy principle proposes a mathematical account of adaptive systems. Variational free energy bounds surprisal; a prediction mismatch is not a complete definition of either quantity.” |
| mind-7-2 | Exactly two exhaustive mechanisms | “Within this framework, perception can update beliefs and action can change incoming sensory evidence. Learning also changes the model over time.” |
| mind-7-3 | Attention simply is precision; fatigue deterministically selects lower-level predictions | “Predictive-processing models describe precision as confidence weighting. Precision weighting is one account of attention; fatigue and stress do not imply a fixed switch to a particular prediction.” |
| mind-7-4 | Every behaviour is proved to serve the body budget | “Allostasis describes anticipating and regulating bodily needs. It is an important perspective on behaviour, not proof that every action has one metabolic explanation.” |
| mind-7-5 | Environmental changes automatically update predictions and behaviour | “You and your environment influence each other. Changing the setting can support a different response, but does not guarantee one.” |
| mind-8-1, mind-8-2, mind-8-4 | Glucose costs explain confirmation bias and refusal to learn | “Learning involves biological change, but metabolic cost alone does not explain confirmation bias or resistance to evidence. Expectations, attention, motivation and how credible the evidence seems can all matter.” |
| mind-8-3 | Expectations mute endorphins and manufacture all confirming evidence | “Expectations can influence what you notice and how you interpret it. Sensory evidence can also challenge expectations; perception is not guaranteed to confirm a belief.” |
| mind-8-5 | Small errors are accepted and large ones rejected because of metabolic price | “A manageable experiment can make practice easier. Whether new evidence changes an expectation depends on more than the size of the mismatch; small steps and major changes can both lead to learning.” |

Evidence basis: expected stimuli can reach awareness, and expectations can facilitate conscious perception ([Melloni et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21273423/)). This directly motivates the mind-1-2 proposal.

The distinction between a theoretical framework and an established exclusive mechanism follows the scope of [Friston, 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2787). These proposals are an editorial inference from that scope, not quotations or evidence that every competing account is settled. Active inference also models exploration and information seeking, so minimizing free energy should not simply be equated with avoiding learning ([Friston et al., 2015](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/Active%20inference%20and%20epistemic%20value.pdf)).

Constructed emotion and allostasis wording should retain attribution to the theory ([Barrett, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5390700/)). Challenges to a limited-resource account of self-control do not establish that self-control itself is unreal ([Inzlicht, Schmeichel and Macrae, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24439530/)).

The practice/streak and metabolic-cost edits remove unsupported guarantees and exclusive causal claims; they are conservative proposed wording, not claims that these sources directly tested every Balance example. No change is proposed here to the already approved mind-6-5 lesson.
