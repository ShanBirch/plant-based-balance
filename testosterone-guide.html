The big picture

Same hormone, different reasons for using it.

Testosterone is both a hormone the body makes naturally and a medication people use externally. At physiological levels it supports normal sexual function, muscle, bone, mood and red-blood-cell production. At higher levels, the same androgen signalling can increase muscle-building potential, strength and training recovery, while also increasing side effects.

During a cut, people are usually interested in testosterone for muscle retention, training output and recovery—not because it directly suppresses appetite. Whether body fat falls still depends mainly on the energy deficit; testosterone changes the body-composition and performance side of that process.

The biology

What testosterone actually does

Testosterone is an androgen hormone. In men, most is made in the testes after signals from the brain and pituitary. It affects sexual function, sperm production, bone, red blood cells, muscle, fat distribution, mood and other tissues.

01

Brain & pituitary

The hypothalamus and pituitary send LH and FSH signals that control testosterone and sperm production.

02

Muscle & bone

Androgen-receptor signalling supports protein synthesis, lean mass, strength adaptation, recovery and bone density. The effect generally becomes more anabolic as exposure rises.

03

Sexual function

Normal testosterone contributes to libido, erections and sperm production—but injected testosterone can reduce or stop sperm production.

04

Conversion

Some testosterone converts to DHT and some to estradiol. Both have useful roles and can also contribute to side effects.

The trade-off

What people want from it—and what comes with it

The effects sit on a spectrum. Restoring a low level can bring someone back toward normal function. Pushing above normal can produce a stronger anabolic effect, but the shutdown of natural production and dose-related side effects also become more relevant.

Why people use testosterone

  • Libido and some aspects of sexual function
  • More potential to build or retain lean mass
  • Bone density over time
  • Strength, training output and recovery
  • Energy, mood or wellbeing in some men
  • Maintenance of normal male sex characteristics

The size of the effect depends on starting testosterone, dose, time, training, food, sleep and individual response. A replacement effect and a performance-enhancing effect are different parts of the same spectrum.

Risks and common trade-offs

  • Reduced sperm production, infertility and smaller testes
  • Raised haematocrit (“thicker” blood) and clot concern
  • Higher blood pressure and fluid retention
  • Acne, oily skin and accelerated male-pattern hair loss
  • Breast tenderness or gynaecomastia
  • Worsened sleep apnoea or urinary symptoms
  • Mood, irritability, anxiety or sleep changes in some people
  • Injection pain, bleeding, infection, abscess or nerve injury

Long-term safety questions remain. Recent indicated-use trial data did not show more major heart attacks or strokes, but pulmonary embolism and fracture signals still require attention.

Dose reality

Dose tells you exposure—not the whole outcome

The exact ester, concentration, injection interval, starting hormone level and individual metabolism all change what a dose produces. Blood pressure, haematocrit, lipids, fertility and side effects show how that exposure is landing in the person—not just the milligrams on the vial.

mg ≠ mg

A large number may be a long-acting dose given every 10–14 weeks, while a smaller number may be a short-acting weekly dose. Injectable products are not automatically interchangeable milligram-for-milligram.

Testosterone enanthate or cypionate Short-acting injection 75–100 mg weekly IM, or 150–200 mg IM every 2 weeks Shorter intervals usually create smaller peaks and troughs. The same weekly amount can produce very different blood levels between people.
Testosterone enanthate auto-injector Product-specific subcutaneous route 75 mg once weekly into the abdominal region; available adjustments are 50, 75 or 100 mg A trough after about 6 weeks shows the exposure produced. This product is designed for subcutaneous use, not IM or intravenous use.

Physiological range

Replacement exposure

The aim is to reproduce a normal testosterone range. Benefits tend to be restoration of normal function, with risks tracked through symptoms and blood markers.

Above physiological

Performance exposure

Higher androgen exposure can produce more muscle and strength effect. Natural production and sperm signalling are suppressed, while haematocrit, blood pressure, acne, hair loss and other effects become more likely.

Compounding risk

High exposure, long duration or stacking

Risk builds with the level produced, time exposed, multiple drugs, uncertain product strength and adverse markers that are allowed to keep rising. There is no single milligram threshold that predicts the outcome for everyone.

Injection routes

Into muscle vs under the skin

Both routes exist, but route and site belong to the exact medicine—not to testosterone in general. A product labelled for deep intramuscular use should not be casually moved into fat, and a subcutaneous auto-injector should not be pushed into muscle.

IM

Intramuscular

The medicine is deposited deeper into a large muscle, where the oil-based ester releases over time.

  • Where: often the gluteal muscle; some products or clinician protocols may use another large muscle.
  • Used for: cypionate, enanthate, mixed esters and long-acting undecanoate, depending on the exact label.
  • Trade-offs: deeper injection, soreness, peaks and troughs with some short-acting schedules.
Some long-acting oil injections must be given in a clinic. Never inject testosterone into a vein.
SC / SubQ

Subcutaneous

The medicine is deposited in the fatty layer beneath the skin. It can be comfortable and produce steadier weekly exposure with a product designed and tested for this route.

  • Where: one approved testosterone-enanthate auto-injector specifies the left or right abdomen, avoiding the area close to the navel.
  • Used for: formulation-specific products; not every oil vial is labelled or tested for subcutaneous use.
  • Trade-offs: redness, bruising, bleeding, firmness or discomfort at the site can occur.
Subcutaneous use still produces systemic testosterone effects. The main difference is how the medicine is deposited and released, not whether it shuts down natural production.

Bloods & monitoring

What the numbers tell you

Bloods separate the assumed effect from the effect actually happening. Baseline results show where someone started; repeat results show the testosterone exposure, the shutdown of natural signalling and whether risk markers are moving.

The useful way to read it

Think in outcomes, exposure and trade-offs—not just a weekly number.

The same dose can land very differently in two people. The goal might be normal function, muscle retention through a cut, strength or recovery. The fuller picture is the result being produced, the blood level and markers behind it, and which side effects or long-term costs come with that result.

A few direct answers

Common questions

These are the practical questions that usually sit behind the conversation: what changes, what does not, and why two people can have different results from the same amount.

Will testosterone make me shredded?

It can help preserve or add lean mass, maintain training output and change how a cut looks, particularly when androgen exposure is higher. It does not create the calorie deficit itself, so fat loss still comes mainly from energy balance. Water and glycogen changes can also make scale weight and visual sharpness move differently.

Does a “low dose” mean low risk?

No. “Low” has no meaning without the formulation, interval, starting level and blood response. A dose that looks modest can still push one person above the normal range, raise haematocrit, increase blood pressure or shut down fertility.

Can natural testosterone production recover after stopping?

It often can, but the timing and completeness are unpredictable. Recovery may take months and can be incomplete, especially after longer or higher-dose exposure. Sperm production can take longer to return than blood testosterone, and fertility may remain suppressed for a period.

Is subcutaneous testosterone safer than intramuscular testosterone?

Not automatically. It may be easier to administer and can create smoother exposure with an appropriate product, but it still has systemic testosterone risks. Route-specific safety depends on the medicine, dose, technique and monitoring.
