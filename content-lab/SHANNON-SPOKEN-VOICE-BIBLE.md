# Shannon Spoken Voice Bible

Read this before writing any Shannon-led spoken script, synthetic Instagram voice note, Reel narration, Story narration, coaching explanation or conversational video line.

This file describes how Shannon forms spoken language. It complements:

- `content-lab/SHANNON-SCRIPT-VOICE-REFERENCE.md` for the approved public-script construction;
- `content-lab/SHANNON-SCRIPT-LIBRARY.md` for exact approved scripts;
- `content-lab/data/shannon-spoken-voice-profile.json` for machine-readable rules and measurements.

## Authority and evidence

Use this order whenever sources disagree:

1. Shannon's latest direct correction.
2. Recent Shannon-approved scripts.
3. Recent outbound Instagram voice notes.
4. Older outbound Instagram voice notes.
5. Generic writing advice.

The source archive contains 537 outbound Instagram audio records under Shannon's two account labels, `Shan_n_Sunny` and `Shannon Birch`. The expanded surface-form pass produced 526 usable transcripts across about 158 minutes. Every usable recording was then speaker-diarized: 512 contained exactly one detected speaker and 14 multi-speaker recordings were excluded entirely. A clean 29-note subset supplied word timing and acoustic measurements. Raw private DM content is not stored in the repository; only aggregates and de-identified constructions are retained.

Automatic speech recognition regularly normalises Australian speech. A transcript that says `How are you going?` is weaker evidence than Shannon directly confirming that he says `How ya going?`.

## Current non-negotiable surface rules

- In public spoken content, write `How ya going?`, not `How are you going?`, when that casual greeting genuinely fits. In generated one-to-one DM voice notes, use Shannon's newer confirmed `Hey, how are ya.` as its own opening breath group, followed by a large pause before the next thought.
- Use a drawn-out `ummm` for Shannon's wondering or thought-searching sound. This is Shannon's direct correction from 2026-08-04 and supersedes the older generic `umm/ahh` rule.
- Do not substitute a relieved `ahh` for that thinking sound. Use `ahh` only when the line genuinely expresses relief or realisation; never auto-insert it as generic filler.
- Put `ummm` at a real thought change. Never use it to cover a missing explanation.
- Use spoken `ya know` after a complete relatable or reassuring point as a soft landing that invites shared understanding. The archive transcript label remains `you know` because recognition normalises the reduction. Do not sprinkle it into unfinished logic or turn every other `you` into `ya`.
- Use the natural contraction whenever one exists: `I'll`, `I'm`, `I've`, `you're`, `you've`, `it's`, `that's`, `there's`, `don't`, `doesn't`, `can't`, `isn't`, `haven't`.
- Do not turn every line into phonetic spelling. The script should guide natural delivery without becoming hard to read or damaging TTS pronunciation.

- `yeah`: 46;
- `morning` or `good morning`: 20;

Use the live relationship and message context:

### New or warm Instagram lead

- Answer the newest detail first when it already provides an opening.
- In a generated one-to-one DM note, use `Hey, how are ya.` when a greeting is useful, then leave a full topic pause. Do not make it a prefix on every reply. Public spoken content may retain `How ya going?`.
- Do not use `bro`, `broski`, `mate` or a person's name without evidence that Shannon already speaks to them that way.
- Keep the first useful question plain. Do not turn it into a survey or a polished coaching prompt.

### Familiar follower, friend or established casual relationship

- `hey`, `yo`, `yeah`, `bro`, playful repetition and abrupt content-first openings become more plausible.
- Preserve the exact address word already established in that thread.
- Let banter remain banter. Do not translate it into coaching language merely because Balance is the business context.

### Client coaching

- Start from the actual problem, then say what Shannon would notice, change, watch or adjust.
- Use `I think`, `you know`, `so`, `yeah` and a measured, drawn-out `ummm` when Shannon is visibly working through the person's situation.
- Do not soften a concrete instruction into vague `support`, `guidance` or `mindfulness` language.

### Science or mechanism explanation

- Keep the causal step correct before adding personality.
- Translate a technical term immediately: `the eccentric, the way down`.
- Use short landing fragments after the complete explanation.
- Put `ummm` before the next thought, not inside the definition or mechanism that must remain clear.

## Ummm, ahh and soft-landing placement

The clean timing subset contained 77 recognisable `um/uh` sounds over 34.68 minutes, or about 2.22 per minute. The surface recogniser under-counted these, which is why acoustic/word-timing evidence is used for frequency.

The archive establishes that hesitation sounds are real. Shannon's later direct listening correction establishes which sound to author by default: `ummm` is the wondering/thought-searching expression; `ahh` sounds like relief and is not interchangeable.

For normal authored delivery:

- 15–30 seconds: usually zero or one core hesitation.
- 30–60 seconds: usually one to three core hesitations.
- 60–90 seconds: usually two to four core hesitations.
- Prefer a long, breathed `ummm` at a genuine thought transition. Do not shorten it into a clipped token between polished clauses.
- Use `ahh` only when the meaning is actually relief or realisation. Do not alternate it mechanically with `ummm` for variety.
- Do not place multiple hesitation sounds inside the same tiny phrase.
- Do not begin every note or Reel with one.
- Let spoken `ya know` follow a complete relatable or reassuring point when a shared-understanding landing is useful: `you don't have to prove anything, ya know.`

Measured delivery:

| Transition | Natural working band | Median |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Clause to clause | 0.18–0.78s | 0.34s |
| Sentence to next thought | 0.66–1.80s | 1.18s |
| Before `umm/ahh` | 0.77–1.93s | 1.40s |
| After `umm/ahh` | 0.28–1.48s | 1.05s |
| Before `so/yeah/well` | 0.24–1.62s | 0.74s |
| After `so/yeah/well` | 0.20–0.52s | 0.35s |
| Repeated-word restart | 0.18–0.77s | 0.45s |

An `umm` lasted about 0.58 seconds at the median, while an ordinary word lasted about 0.26 seconds. Shannon's approved authored form may be drawn out further as `ummm`; the sound needs time and breath around it. A fast `umm` inserted between polished clauses does not reproduce Shannon's cadence.

## Sentence construction

- Overall pace: about 122 words per minute.
- Typical note: 111–139 words per minute.
- Median sentence: 13 words.
- About 22% of sentences were four words or fewer.
- About 25% of sentences were questions.
- Median breath group: 9 words; 75% were 15 words or fewer.

The characteristic structure is a complete idea built from smaller spoken groups:

1. Direct setup.
2. One useful explanatory line.
3. A pause, `so`, `yeah` or a drawn-out `ummm` at the real turn.
4. A short restatement or concrete picture.
5. The next state.

Sentence starts were led by `so`, `and`, `I`, `hey`, `yeah`, `but`, `it's`, `yo` and `like`. Beginning with `and` or `but` is natural when it continues the live thought. It is not a mistake to repair into formal prose.

## Reductions: use, do not costume

### Canonical

- `How ya going?`
- all normal contractions;
- a drawn-out `ummm` as the default wondering or thought-searching beat;
- `ahh` only for genuine relief or realisation;
- spoken `ya know` as a shared-understanding soft landing after a complete thought.

### Supported when relaxed

- `gonna`, `wanna`, `gotta`, `kinda`, `dunno`, `reckon`;
- `goin'` as a delivery direction when the speech engine otherwise becomes too formal.

### Context-bound

- `bro`, `broski`, `mate`, `man`, `yo`;
- profanity;
- `cos`, which appears more in older than recent clips.

Do not globally change every `you` to `ya`, every `going to` to `gonna`, or every `think` to `reckon`. That produces an Australian costume rather than Shannon.

## Recent speech outweighs old speech

Current direct corrections always win. Where Shannon has not corrected a form, weight 2025–2026 clips above 2023–2024, then 2020–2022.

- `goin'` rose from 0.43 to 4.16 uses per 1,000 words in the old-versus-recent comparison.
- relationship-specific `bro` rose from 0.22 to 13.77 per 1,000 words.
- `right` as a check or landing rose from 1.94 to 4.81 per 1,000 words.
- `cos` fell from 1.72 to zero in the recent corpus.
- `wanna` and heavy `just` usage are also older-weighted.

Therefore: preserve `How ya going?`, natural `goin'` delivery, thread-earned `bro`, and occasional `right`; do not revive `cos`, `wanna` or old softener density merely because they exist somewhere in the archive.

## Script QA

Run:

```powershell
npm run voice:check -- --file path\to\script.txt --context dm
```

Supported contexts include `dm`, `new_lead`, `spoken`, `reel` and `science`. The checker rejects expanded contractions and formal greeting/presenter phrases, warns about a missing core hesitation in longer scripts, catches stacked hesitation sounds, checks sentence shape and protects relationship-specific words. A passing `ahh` still needs human semantic review because it is not the default thinking sound.

The checker is a first gate, not proof by itself. A passing script must still match the exact relationship, topic, current facts and closest approved example.
