# Shannon Content Edit Memory

Read this before creating or revising Balance feed posts, Stories, Reels, carousels, covers or their automations.

For any Shannon-led spoken script, also read `content-lab/SHANNON-SCRIPT-VOICE-REFERENCE.md`. Its cravings script is the canonical voice and sentence-construction example. Match how it sounds; do not assume every future Reel should reuse its topic or three-step structure.

When Shannon gives explicit edit feedback, treat it as a reusable production rule unless he clearly says it applies only once. Record the original direction, translate it into a scalable rule, and update the relevant skill or automation contract. Do not leave important visual or voice preferences trapped inside one preview folder or chat.

## Active production rules

### Real Shannon media first

- Shannon-led coaching and food carousels use approved real Shannon photos when suitable images exist.
- Source from `C:/Users/shann/OneDrive/Desktop/desktop desktop/photos of shannon` before project copies, stock media, generated people or abstract artwork.
- Use different, visibly distinct photos across one carousel. Include both professional and candid images when available.
- Preserve photo provenance in the campaign brief or handoff.

### Every visual must earn its place

- Do not add arbitrary circles, rings, blobs, pills or decorative geometry merely to fill space.
- Shapes are allowed only when they encode a clear relationship, quantity, sequence or brand function that a viewer can understand immediately.
- For food and coaching education, prefer relevant real photography, recognisable food imagery or concise typography over abstract stand-ins.
- If a visual makes the idea stranger or less clear, remove it.

### Keep sensory-input coaching examples broad

- When a post is teaching that behaviour changes with sensory or environmental inputs, don't prematurely reduce the explanation to food intake, hunger or a nutrition fix.
- Prefer a simple context change the viewer can picture, such as changing rooms at the specific time the pattern normally occurs.
- Land the example with Shannon's preferred mechanism line: changing any input will change the output.

### Balance carousel logo shimmer

- Balance carousel posts default to five-second MP4 slides rather than still-only delivery, including photo-first coaching carousels, unless Shannon explicitly requests still images.
- Use the approved large bottom-right Balance kanji/ring watermark with the white disc removed.
- Keep the base mark quiet black at about 17% opacity. Run two brief diagonal gold passes through the logo mask beginning at 1.20 and 3.55 seconds, each lasting 0.65 seconds, then return completely to black.
- The shimmer belongs to the logo itself. Do not substitute a corner sparkle, moving gold bar, permanent gold logo, pulse, rotation, particles or whole-slide glow.
- Composite copy above the watermark and keep the mark and sheen clear of Shannon's face and head.
- Carousel preview pages must play the final MP4 slides inline; poster JPGs alone cannot prove the shimmer.

### Balance carousel slide rhythm

- Balance feed carousels can use five to ten slides. Choose the slide count from the number of story or teaching beats needed for a cold viewer to understand the post; never force a complete idea into exactly five slides.
- Keep the established alternating visual language: full-bleed photo or real evidence media, then a cream Canva-style editorial card when explanation, reframe or action is needed. Five-slide posts may use photo, Canva, photo, Canva, photo, but that is a useful rhythm rather than a fixed template.
- Photo slides carry one large visual beat with short overlay copy. Cream cards carry explanation, reframe or practical action using centred charcoal type, one Balance-gold phrase, a short gold rule, generous whitespace and the large low-opacity Balance watermark.
- Do not build a normal Balance teaching or proof carousel as a long run of photos with separate caption-like overlays. The alternating rhythm is what makes the story feel intentional and readable.
- For media-backed client proof, actual client video can occupy a media beat whenever it is the strongest receipt. Use additional slides, up to ten, when the timeline, evidence or coaching interpretation would otherwise be unclear.

### Shannon's face is protected

- Never place text, captions, cards, diagrams, badges, logos, CTAs or decorative motion over Shannon's face, mouth, eyes, jawline, hairline or head silhouette.
- In graphic-led Reels, use the approved Part Two circular presenter bubble when Shannon needs to remain visible.
- In photo carousels, place copy in genuine negative space using a local translucent field; do not hide most of the photo behind an opaque panel.

### Face privacy must never look censored

- Never place a visible square, rectangle, black patch, opaque block, hard-edged blur or pixel tile over a client's face.
- When anonymising real Story media, use a tightly fitted feathered oval/circular blur that blends naturally into the frame, or choose a safe crop, soft background treatment, still frame or branded proof card.
- Inspect native-resolution frames across the full clip. If the treatment looks pasted on, has a box edge or obscures unrelated body/equipment detail, reject it and re-render.

### Client proof should show the receipts

- Build feed proof as a visual sequence from the strongest real media available: the result, the earlier decision or check-in, the logged workout/meal details, and the relevant photo or footage.
- Don't turn a media-backed client story into a run of black branded cards. Use an editorial text card only for a genuine beat that has no attached media, and never invent a missing photo.
- When a client's face appears in Balance feed media, keep the creative approval-only until public Instagram consent is confirmed.
- Every proof carousel must make sense to a cold viewer without the caption. The opening slide names the person and states the before/after hook; later slides use explicit timeline or evidence labels such as `THE NIGHT BEFORE`, `THE NEXT DAY`, `THE ACTUAL SESSION`, and `THE COACHING BIT` so the viewer never has to infer what an image means.
- Do not place several receipts in sequence and assume the viewer will connect them. Each photo or video needs one short line explaining its role in the story, and the final coaching slide must state what the evidence does and does not prove.
- Match the large, phone-readable hierarchy of the approved cravings coaching carousel. At 1080x1350, default to roughly 52-60 px bold story headlines, 28-36 px supporting copy, and 22-26 px labels. Do not use caption-sized explanation text on the artwork; shorten the line or split the story across another slide instead of shrinking it.
- Covers must also pass a profile-grid test, not only a full-screen carousel test. Use a short hook with roughly 72-86 px bold type at 1080x1350, keep the core hook inside the central grid-safe crop, and verify it remains readable at profile-thumbnail size. Shorten the cover before reducing the font.

### Coaching posts must show how Shannon coaches

- A coaching carousel cannot stop at explaining the mechanism and then attach a coaching CTA. It must show what Shannon actually does with the person.
- Use a clear coaching process: remove blame, notice the live problem, investigate the repeating setup, choose one useful change, observe what happens, and adjust again.
- Write in Shannon's coaching language: `we work out`, `we look at`, `we change`, `we see what changes`, `we learn from it`, and `we adjust`. Avoid vague phrases such as `support`, `accountability`, or `personalised guidance` when the post can name the real coaching action.
- The Balance bridge should make the offer concrete: Shannon helps identify what's driving the problem, changes the setup with the person, and keeps them moving without blame.
- When `input -> output` feels abstract, use a familiar bodily analogy before the coaching steps. Shannon's approved cravings example is hearing running water and suddenly feeling the urge to wee: running water is the input; the urge is the output. Keep the mapping explicit and do not imply every craving has one simple cause.

### Shannon's spoken delivery

- Natural contractions are mandatory in Shannon's spoken and public-facing copy wherever one exists. Write forms such as `I'll`, `you're`, `we're`, `it's`, `that's`, `there's`, `don't`, `doesn't`, `isn't`, `can't`, `won't`, `haven't`, and `hasn't`; never expand them into presenter-style wording. Preserve an expanded form only inside an exact quotation that cannot be changed.
- Do not insert `um` or `ah` fillers into Shannon's scripts. Keep the delivery natural with phrases such as `so yeah`, `okay`, `honestly`, pauses, fragments, and an occasional restart or self-correction.
- Use the approved cravings cadence deliberately: a direct setup such as `Alright` or `Okay, ready?`, intentional pause space, a mix of complete explanations and clipped fragments, then a repeated or compressed line that wraps each important idea. Natural does not mean adding filler; it means giving the thought somewhere to land.
- Imperfect beats must sound like Shannon's real phrasing, not a synthetic checklist of filler words.
- Use the recovery-meal Reel as the main raw-voice anchor: abrupt lines, direct audience talk, useful mechanism, mock frustration or profanity, an odd little aside, and a CTA that still sounds like Shannon. The science can be exact and the edit can be polished; the speech should not sound like a documentary, journal abstract, lesson, or tidy presenter script.
- Avoid formal transitions such as `This demonstrates`, `But it does show`, `The practical takeaway is`, and `It is important to understand`. Put formal wording only around the evidence, then translate it immediately into ordinary speech.
- HeyGen is used for precision lip-sync of an approved reusable Shannon clip, not complete Reel generation.
- The durable paid-production order is approved script, approved ElevenLabs voice, verified real duration, approved reusable presenter source, HeyGen `precision` lip-sync, then local Reel composition. Check the installed HeyGen connector first and use the authenticated CLI fallback when necessary. Authentication approval remains a visible Shannon action; do not automate it.
- Voice-production scripts must be ASCII-safe or read with explicit UTF-8 encoding before ElevenLabs. Before any paid HeyGen generation, transcribe the complete voice file and fail the gate on mojibake, invented `TM`/`TMs`/`TMM` sounds, damaged contractions, or a missing CTA.
- Measure narration loudness on both sides of every scene transition, especially graphic-slide handoffs. Level the complete voice track before the final mix and reject any section where the voice drops noticeably or the music masks it; visual changes must never coincide with an accidental dialogue-volume change.
- When a Reel opens with a viewer question, write it as a complete spoken question and direct the voice with an audible lift, energy, and rising question contour. Reject monotone hooks before lip-sync; approve a short isolated hook take first when delivery is subjective.
- Shannon-led Reel narration must perform the emotional job of each beat rather than hold one neutral tone across the script. Map delivery intentionally: energetic hook, engaged explanation, firm blame removal, brisk actionable section, playful or personal proof, and warm confident CTA. Generate long narration in coherent segments with surrounding-text continuity when needed, then level loudness without flattening the prosody. Reject a generally monotone full take before paid lip-sync.
- Treat the approved final cravings Reel voice from 2026-07-29 as the ElevenLabs performance benchmark for future Reels. Match its energetic question hook, clear emotional changes between teaching, relief, action and story beats, personality in the personal example, and warm confident CTA. Do not fall back to a single neutral delivery merely because the transcript is correct.
- Treat music as part of the emotional storytelling, not a token background layer. Build a deliberate score arc across every Reel: tension and curiosity in the hook, focus under teaching, relief at the blame-removal beat, momentum through actions, personality in personal proof, a lift into the summary, and a warm confident resolution through the CTA.
- Use purposeful SFX on major cognitive and emotional turns: hook impact, scene sweeps, mechanism reveals, numbered actions, contrast or punchline accents, summary lock-in and CTA arrival. Do not hit every caption. “Go hard” means bold, intentional sound design whose effects remain subordinate to the spoken message, not indiscriminate loudness.
- Duck music beneath dense claims, personal punchlines and the CTA, then let it rise in transitions and emotional releases. Validate the final mix on a phone: narration must remain dominant and even, while music and major effects must still be clearly perceptible through phone speakers.
- Segmented narration must sound continuous. Measure the real speech end of every generated segment, trim hidden trailing silence, and use one short controlled join pause; never stack an added pause on top of a clip's built-in tail. Reject random dropouts or dead-air joins before voice approval.
- When the complete approved script fits one ElevenLabs request and the presenter source can hold the result, prefer one continuous generation with one fixed voice, model, seed and settings. Do not splice separately styled sections merely to force emotion when that makes Shannon's apparent voice identity change across the Reel. Use one-take delivery plus whole-file level control; segment only when provider limits or a failed section make it genuinely necessary.
- Audit every generated narration segment individually for background noise, breaths, clicks, hiss, room tone, and voice artifacts before assembly. Regenerate only the contaminated segment with surrounding-text continuity, then rerun the full transcript, silence, and level checks.
- Entertainment must come from meaningful visual state changes, not random decoration. For exercise Reels, target a relevant visual change about every two seconds and reject long talking-head-plus-loop holds even when captions are moving. Rotate between live movement proof, angle/crop changes, rep freezes, anatomical mechanism inserts, force-direction arrows, side-by-side comparisons, cue targets and coaching-progress reveals. Generated visuals must teach the exact spoken mechanism and stay brief; generic anatomy, decorative AI imagery and unrelated motion are still slop. Keep one dominant focal idea per moment and protect Shannon's face.
- Exercise Reels may open on a full-screen Shannon/avatar when the human hook is the strongest first beat. Fill the portrait frame cleanly; reject narrow presenter panels, avoidable blurred side bars and excessive empty space. Move to full-screen exercise whenever the movement is the proof, using a protected circular presenter bubble only when Shannon's presence adds value without hiding the relevant joints or equipment. Rotate approved avatar looks so the series does not overuse one character.
- Personal examples must explain the contrast in a complete thought before the punchline. Prefer a specific, recognisably Shannon detail over a vague one-word payoff. When the contrast is counterintuitive, acknowledge that it is strange to comprehend before landing the clipped mechanism summary; for the cravings reference, use the mate's-place/McDonald's example, then `So yeah...`, then `Same body. Different environment. Different signals. Different output.`
- Conversion-focused Balance Reels inherit the science-Reel evidence, face-protection, voice, edit and QA standards, then add a clearer problem, coaching reframe, useful solution, future identity and one small CTA.
